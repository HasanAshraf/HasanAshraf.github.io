About

I work on motion planning for industrial robots that build, weld and finish metal parts.

I'm a PhD student in Mechanical Engineering at the University of Alberta, advised by Dr. Ahmed Jawad Qureshi in the Additive Design and Manufacturing Systems (ADaMS) Lab. My research is on constraint-aware motion and trajectory planning, collision avoidance, and coordinating several robots that share one workspace and one process.

Most of that work happens on a 14-DOF hybrid manufacturing cell: two 6-DOF industrial robots sharing a 2-DOF tilt–rotate positioner, used for wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM), welding, grinding and forging. I'm also a Mitacs Research Graduate Intern with Elementiam, where I build optimization-based welding trajectories and a perception pipeline for real-time collision avoidance.

Before Alberta, I earned a B.E. in Mechatronics Engineering from NUST in Pakistan and spent a semester at Kennesaw State University as a Global UGRAD exchange scholar.

Research interests

Projects

  1. 2026

    Articulated4D: generative modeling of articulated assets

    Used state-flow matching to synthesize 4D temporal sequences from static 2D images, with a latent-diffusion training pipeline on NVIDIA H100 clusters focused on joint-limit consistency in the generated meshes.

  2. 2025

    Vehicle state estimation with GPS–IMU sensor fusion

    A Kalman filter for vehicle localization with a χ² innovation gate to reject GPS interference, reaching 1.9 m RMSE on the KITTI dataset.

  3. 2025

    Anomaly detection via reverse distillation with balanced few-shot mask supervision

    Extended RD4AD with pixel-wise few-shot supervision and a mask-aligned feature loss to find medical parasites under severe foreground–background imbalance. A push–pull feature learning strategy improved anomaly localization by up to 25% pixel-AUPRC over few-shot baselines.
